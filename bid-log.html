A Simple Bid Log for New Contractors

When you're starting out, bids live in your inbox and your head. That works until six are out at once and you can't remember which GC you owe a call. A bid log is one row per bid and about five minutes a week.

What to track

  • Bid number, client and contact
  • Project, location and your scope
  • Includes and excludes: what your price covers and what it doesn't
  • Bid due date and the date you sent it
  • Last contact and the next follow-up date
  • Your cost, your markup and the price you quoted
  • Status: preparing, sent, won, lost or no bid

A follow-up rhythm

When you send a bid, set a follow-up date about a week out, and reset it every time you talk to the client. A short check-in ("Any questions on our scope? Anything you need from us?") keeps your number in the conversation, and you often learn where you stand.

When a bid is decided, mark it won or lost and note why if you can. After 15 or 20 bids, your win rate and those notes tell you which work to chase.

Spell out includes and excludes on every proposal

Most scope fights start with something nobody wrote down. List what's in your price and what isn't (permits, patching, disposal, after-hours work) right on the proposal, and give the price a valid-until date.
