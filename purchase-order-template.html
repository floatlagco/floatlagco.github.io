Purchase Orders for Subcontractors: What to Include

Plenty of small subs order material by phone or email and hope the invoice matches. A one-page purchase order fixes most of that: price, quantity and delivery date are in writing before anything ships.

What goes on a PO

  • A PO number tied to the job. Job number, then a sequence (2026-014-003), so every invoice traces back to a job.
  • Vendor and contact. Who quoted it, and the quote number if there is one.
  • Ship-to. The job site address, the gate or entrance to use, and a site contact with a phone number.
  • Need date. The date it has to be on site, worked back from the schedule (here's how to figure it).
  • Line items. Description, quantity, unit, unit price and extended price. Reference the approved submittal on the lines it covers, so the supplier ships what was approved.
  • Tax, freight and total. Say whether sales tax applies and who pays freight.
  • Terms. Payment terms, delivery notice ("call 24 hours before delivery"), and a line that changes to price or quantity need a revised PO.
  • Signature. Who authorized it, and the date.

Keep a PO log

One row per PO: number, vendor, date issued, need date, total, and whether it's been delivered. Once a week, sort by need date and call about anything due in the next two weeks that doesn't have a confirmed ship date.
