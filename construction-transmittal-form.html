Construction Transmittal Form: What to Include

A transmittal is the cover sheet that rides on top of anything you send: shop drawings, product data, samples, O&M manuals, a revised proposal. It takes two minutes to fill out, and it answers the question that comes up later in every dispute: what did you send, to whom, and when?

What goes on it

  • Transmittal number. Your own running sequence for the job. Reference it in your submittal log so the two connect.
  • Date, project and job number. The project name the way the GC writes it, plus your internal number.
  • To and from. Company, person and email. Send it to the person named in the GC's procedures, not whoever answered the phone last.
  • Items sent. One line per item: description, spec section, sheet or submittal number, number of copies, and the date or revision on it.
  • Sent for. Check one: approval, review and comment, your use, record, or as requested. "Approval" starts a review clock; "your use" does not.
  • Sent by. Email, file share link, mail, courier or hand delivery. For a physical sample, who signed for it.
  • Action requested and response due. The date you need it back, tied to the order-by date it holds up.
  • Remarks. Anything the reviewer needs to know: deviations from the spec, a substitution, or a resubmittal with what changed.

Habits that pay off

  • One transmittal per package. Don't bury a resubmittal inside a batch of new items; it gets missed.
  • Call out what changed. On a resubmittal, list each comment and how you answered it. Reviewers turn those around faster.
  • Keep the sent copy. Save the transmittal with the attachments exactly as sent. If a reviewer says they never got the revised sheet, you have the record.
  • Log it the same day. Put the transmittal number and response due date in your submittal log, then check open items every week. A due date you never worked back from the schedule is just a guess.

Transmittal or submittal?

The submittal is the thing being reviewed. The transmittal is the record of handing it over. Many GCs have their own submittal cover sheet; use theirs when the contract asks for it, and keep your transmittal number on it anyway so your log stays in one sequence.
