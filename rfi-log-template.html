RFI Log Template for Subcontractors: What to Track

An RFI that sits unanswered for three weeks can stop a crew. Most of the time it isn't ignored; it's lost. A simple log, checked once a week, keeps every open question in front of you and gives you the dates you need if it turns into a delay or a change order.

What to track

  • RFI number. Your own sequence, plus the GC's number if their system assigns one.
  • Question. One question per RFI, in a sentence or two.
  • Reference. Drawing sheet, detail and spec section.
  • Date sent and response due. Use the response time in your contract or the GC's procedures; if there isn't one, ask for a date when you send it.
  • Date answered and days open. Days open is the number that matters in a delay conversation.
  • Cost impact and schedule impact. A simple yes or no on each. A yes on either is a flag to price a change order.
  • Status. Open, answered, or closed into a change order.

Write RFIs that get answered

  • Ask one thing. Three questions in one RFI usually get one answer.
  • Propose a solution. "Detail 4/A501 conflicts with the duct at grid C; we propose dropping the header 4 inches. Please confirm." A yes or no comes back faster than an open question.
  • Attach what helps. A marked-up sheet or a field photo saves a round of back and forth.
  • Say when you need it. Tie the response due date to the work it holds up.

Check it every week

Sort open RFIs by response due date. Anything past due gets a call, not another email. When an answer adds cost or time, open a change order request the same week and reference the RFI number on it, so the paper trail connects.
