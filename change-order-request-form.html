What to Put on a Change Order Request

The change order requests that get signed fast are the ones a GC can approve without calling you. They answer the questions up front: what changed, why, what it costs, and whether it moves the schedule.

The fields

  • Number and date. Number every request (CO-001, CO-002), even the ones that get rejected, so your log has no gaps.
  • Reference. The RFI, directive, drawing revision or field condition behind it. This is the "why," and it's the first thing a reviewer checks.
  • Description of the change. What you'll do, in plain words, with quantities where they help. "Add panic devices at the two stair doors per RFI-002" beats "door hardware changes."
  • Cost breakdown. Labor, material, equipment and subcontractors on separate lines. A single lump number invites a request for backup.
  • Markup. Its own line, at the rate your contract allows.
  • Time. Calendar days added, or "no change to contract time." If you leave time off, it can be read as giving it up.
  • Approval block. A signature and date line for the GC, plus a line saying work on the change isn't authorized until it's approved in writing.

Habits that get them paid

  • Send it fast. Many contracts set a notice period for changes. Read yours, and put the deadline on your calendar the day the change comes up.
  • Track what's pending. A request nobody follows up on can sit until closeout, when you have the least leverage.
  • Start work before pricing is done? Track it as time and materials, with daily sheets the GC's super signs.
